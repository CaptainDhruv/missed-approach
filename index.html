Missed Approach Simulator

Fly an approach to minima, go around, and watch the aircraft move through the initial, intermediate and final missed approach phases. The simulator checks your climb against the 30 m and 50 m obstacle clearance (MOC) protection. Try an engine-out climb or a late go-around to see protection being lost.

What the simulator models

  • Initial phase: MAPt to SOC, no turns. The design assumes no climb here, so SOC = MAPt + MAPt tolerance + transitional tolerance X (15 s of flight).
  • Intermediate phase: from SOC, straight climb at the procedure gradient with 30 m (98 ft) MOC. It ends where 50 m (164 ft) MOC is first obtained and can be maintained.
  • Final phase: 50 m MOC (40 m for Cat H), up to the missed approach altitude, where holding, a new approach or en-route flight begins.
  • After a go-around the aircraft keeps sinking briefly (height loss) while it pitches up, then climbs at its performance gradient. If that gradient is below the published one, the aircraft eats into the protection margin.
  • Return & land: after levelling at the missed approach altitude, the aircraft holds, turns back outbound, descends, turns inbound and flies a second approach. This time it gets visual reference at minima and lands. You can press GO AROUND again during the second approach.
  • This is a simplified teaching model. The MAPt tolerance, straight missed approach only, and a single obstacle are approximations. Real procedures use the full Doc 8168 Vol II construction.
